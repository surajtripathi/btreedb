known limitations deferred, not forgotten
5 open ▶
split!Split is not crash-atomic across page writes. A leaf split writes the left half back to the original page and the right half to a new page, then propagates a parent update. A crash after the left/right writes land but before the parent (or superblock, for a root split) is updated leaves the right half's data unreachable — silent loss of previously-committed keys, not just the operation in flight. WAL replay alone does not fix this, since it only redoes the triggering operation, not data that was already durable before the split began. Real fix: never overwrite the original leaf's page until the entire split (new page + parent link) is fully durable, so the original stays a valid single source of truth until its replacement is confirmed safe.
keys!A single key/value larger than roughly half a page can make a split still overflow, or fail to fit even in a fresh empty leaf/internal node. Not handled — encode() surfaces ErrorOverFlow rather than corrupting data, but Put won't give a clean "key too large" message in this case.
rebuild!Every insert fully decodes and re-encodes the entire page rather than editing the slot directory and cell data in place. Correct and simple, but not what real slotted-page engines do at scale. Flagged as a concrete before/after optimization for the benchmark step, not fixed now.
n/n+1!The invariant len(children) == len(keys)+1 on InternalNode is never explicitly validated — enforced by construction so far, but nothing rejects a malformed node if that ever breaks. Also: an even merged key count during splitInternal produces a slightly uneven (e.g. 2-1) split — correct, just not balanced.
delete!Delete has no underflow handling — a leaf or internal node can shrink arbitrarily small (even to zero real entries) without merging with a sibling or borrowing from one. Correct, just not space-efficient. Real systems (Postgres included) often defer this too, via lazy delete + later vacuum/cleanup, rather than eager rebalancing on every delete.

step 10 Benchmarks + written comparison
v1.0 ▶

Full suite against kvstore (the LSM-tree). Headline finding: Get-miss is ~18x faster on the LSM-tree via its bloom filter — btreedb has no early-exit mechanism, hit and miss cost the same. Put allocations (256 vs 6 allocs/op) tell a truer story than near-identical wall-clock time. Range scans amortize well (5.6x cheaper per-entry at 5,000 vs 10 keys) — the clearest B-tree structural win, not benchmarked on the LSM side.

·✓Put/Get-hit/Get-miss/Delete/RangeGet benchmarks, fixed -benchtime=Nx methodology throughout
·✓Caught and fixed two benchmark artifacts: auto-scaled b.N hiding a real Put comparison, and mismatched key shapes producing a fake Get hit/miss gap
·✓Shallow-vs-deep tree Put comparison: allocation cost tracks entries-per-leaf, not tree depth — concrete evidence for the deferred full-rebuild optimization
·✓Full written comparison against kvstore, with results table and explained findings, not just numbers
